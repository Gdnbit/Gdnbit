# Work

## Enterprise AI delivery

I work on turning real business requirements into usable internal systems with AI-assisted development.

The public description is intentionally anonymized. Client names, internal repository names, screenshots, business data, contracts and identifying implementation details are not published here.

### What I actually do

In my current enterprise work, requirements are generally collected or proposed by client-side departments. My responsibility starts from turning those requirements into something that can be built and used:

```text
Requirement interpretation
        ↓
Business rules & edge cases
        ↓
Workflow / product design
        ↓
Data, API & interaction contracts
        ↓
AI-assisted implementation
        ↓
Validation, iteration & delivery
```

Typical work includes understanding an existing process, resolving ambiguous rules, defining inputs/outputs and states, checking whether the implementation matches the intended workflow, and iterating with users or stakeholders.

I have also participated in rapid MVP work where requirements were gathered end-to-end and converted into a focused working prototype before any full-scale rollout decision.

## How I use coding agents

Coding agents are a core part of my development workflow, not an occasional autocomplete layer.

I spend more attention on:

- problem framing and workflow decomposition;
- product/system boundaries and contracts;
- repository context and implementation instructions;
- acceptance criteria, tests and failure cases;
- reviewing outputs and driving iteration;
- connecting software behavior back to the actual business process.

This is why I describe my work closer to **AI Product Engineering / Agentic Product Building / Enterprise AI Delivery** than to a traditional code-volume-oriented developer role.

## What I do not claim

I do not treat every repository inside an organization as my work, and I do not present private client systems as open-source proof.

The public portfolio separates:

- work I directly contributed to;
- personal projects I designed or built;
- public repositories that can be independently inspected;
- private/building projects that are clearly labeled as such.
